Welcome to My Town

Prepare at home  ·  present to the class next week
Name:
WhatA short talk about a town
How long2 to 3 minutes
BringA simple map, drawn by hand
WhenOur next class
Why we are doing this We do not have a second class this week, so this project takes its place. Everything you need is language you already have from this week — there is, there are, and the prepositions of place. This is your chance to use all of it at once, in your own words.

1Choose your town

Tap one. All three are equally good, and nobody will know which one you chose unless you say.

2What your talk must include

  1. The name of your town, and one sentence about it. Welcome to Sangbar. It is a small town between two mountains.
  2. At least five places, using there is and there are. There is a market in the center. There are two schools.
  3. Where things are — at least four prepositions of place. next to · between · across from · in front of · behind · near · at the end of
  4. One thing that makes it special — something no other town has. In our town there is a bridge made of glass.
  5. A question for the class at the end. Would you like to live in my town? Why?
Your map Draw it by hand on a piece of paper and hold it up to the camera. It does not need to be beautiful — boxes with names in them are perfect. If you cannot show a drawing, type the list of places in the chat instead. You do not need any app.
If you want more — B1+
  • Say what your town does not have, using there isn't or there aren't any.
  • Give directions from one place to another. The phrases are in the box below.
  • Answer the class's questions without looking at your notes.

3Language you can use

Starting and finishing Welcome to…  ·  Today I want to tell you about…  ·  First…  ·  Next to that…  ·  Also…  ·  Finally…  ·  Thank you. Do you have any questions?
Saying what is there There is a…  ·  There are two…  ·  There isn't any…  ·  There aren't any…  ·  In the center there is…  ·  It is next to…  ·  It is across from…  ·  At the end of the street there is…
Giving directions — for B1+ Go straight ahead.  ·  Turn left. / Turn right.  ·  Take the first street on your right.  ·  Go past the school.  ·  It is on your left.  ·  It is at the end of the road.

5What a good talk looks like

This is what your teacher and your classmates will be listening for.

We will listen forWhat that means
You used the grammar At least five sentences with there is or there are, and four prepositions of place.
We could follow it You said things in an order that made sense, and we could picture the town.
You talked, not read You looked up from your notes. Pauses are fine. Reading every word is not.
You answered a question Somebody asked you something, and you answered in a full sentence.
Practice out loud, at least twice Reading your notes silently is not practice. Say the whole thing out loud, to yourself, to a family member, or into your phone. The second time is always better than the first, and the class will hear the difference.
If you are nervous Everyone is. You may hold your notes, you may pause, and you may say “Sorry, let me start that again.” — that is real English and nobody will mind. Three minutes is shorter than it sounds.
