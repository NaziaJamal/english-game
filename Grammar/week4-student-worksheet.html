B2 · At the market

Complete the conversation with some, any, much, How much or How many. Then read it with your partner.

Shopkeeper:Good morning. Can I help you?
Customer:Yes, please. I need rice.
Shopkeeper:How do you need?
Customer:Two kilos, please. And do you have tomatoes?
Shopkeeper:Yes, we have very nice ones today.
Customer: should I take for four people?
Shopkeeper:About six.
Customer:Thank you. is that altogether?
Shopkeeper:That's two hundred and fifty.

CWriting on your ownDo this at home and bring it to the next class

C1 · What is in your kitchen?

Write five sentences about the food in your kitchen right now. Use some, any, much, many and a lot of — one in each sentence.

For example We have a lot of rice.  ·  There isn't much sugar.  ·  We haven't got any eggs.

C2 · A meal you love

Think of a meal you love. Write a shopping list for it, then write three sentences saying why you chose it and who you would cook it for.

Challenge — if you want to go further Use a few with things you can count, and a little with things you cannot: “a few onions”, “a little salt”.
DPair and group workSpeaking together in class

D1 · Role-play: at the market

Work in pairs. One of you is the customer, the other is the shopkeeper. Do not look at your partner's card. Then swap roles and do it again.

Student A

You are the customer

  • You are cooking dinner for six people tonight.
  • You need: rice, tomatoes, onions, oil and bread.
  • Ask how much everything costs before you agree.
  • You do not have much money, so ask the price of each thing.
You can say Do you have any…?  ·  I need some…
How much is…?  ·  How many should I take?
That's too expensive.  ·  I'll take two kilos, please.
Student B

You are the shopkeeper

  • You have a small shop in the market.
  • You have a lot of rice, onions and bread today.
  • You have only a few tomatoes left, and no oil at all.
  • Offer the customer something else instead of the oil.
You can say Can I help you?  ·  How much would you like?
I'm sorry, we haven't got any…
Would you like some… instead?  ·  That's… altogether.
Second time — make it harder Cover the sentence starters and do the role-play again without them. Add one problem of your own: the shop has run out of something else, or you want to ask for a better price.

EClass discussionAll together — there are no right answers here

Talk about these questions as a class. Listen to your classmates and ask them one question each.

  1. Is it better to cook at home or to buy food that is already made? Why?
  2. What food from your region would you want everyone in the world to try?
  3. Who taught you to cook? What was the first thing you learned to make?
  4. Do you plan your shopping, or decide when you get to the market?
  5. Is there a food you could eat every day and never get tired of?
While you listen Choose one classmate's answer and ask them a follow-up question: Why?  How often do you make it?  How many people do you cook for?
