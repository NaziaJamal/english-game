Adverbs of Frequency

Week 3 — Daily Life & Time  ·  Saying how often something happens

1What are adverbs of frequency?

Adverbs of frequency tell us how often something happens. We use them very often with the present simple, to talk about habits and routines.[1]

  • I always drink tea in the morning.
  • She never eats meat.
  • We usually have class on Friday.

2The frequency scale

These are the most common adverbs, from the most often to the least often.[1][2]

always
100%
every time — She always arrives early.
usually
about 90%
most times — I usually walk to work.
often
about 70%
many times — We often cook together.
sometimes
about 50%
now and then — He sometimes works late.
rarely / seldom
about 10%
almost never — They rarely watch TV.
never
0%
no times — I never forget her birthday.

You may also meet: occasionally (about 30%), frequently (about 70%), and hardly ever (about 5%).

3Where does the adverb go?

This is the most important rule in this handout. There are three situations, and they are easy once you see the pattern.[2][3]

Rule 1 — Before the main verb

In simple tenses, the adverb goes between the subject and the main verb.

SubjectAdverbVerb + rest
Shealwaysdrinks coffee in the morning.
Theynevereat meat.
My brotherusuallystudies at night.

Rule 2 — After the verb be

When the verb is am, is, are, was, were, the adverb comes after it, not before.

SubjectbeAdverb + rest
Sheisalways on time.
Heisnever late.
Theyareusually at home in the evening.
Compare these two She always drinks tea.  (main verb → adverb goes before)
She is always tired.  (the verb be → adverb goes after)

Rule 3 — After the first helping verb

If there is a helping verb (have, will, can, do), the adverb goes after the first helping verb and before the main verb.

  • I have never been to Kabul.
  • You can always ask me for help.
  • I will never forget your kindness.
  • We don't usually work on Friday.

4In negatives and questions

Negatives

The adverb goes after don't or doesn't.

  • I don't usually eat breakfast.
  • He doesn't often speak in class.
Careful with "never" Never is already negative, so we do not use it with don't.
Not correctI don't never eat meat.
CorrectI never eat meat.

Questions

The adverb goes after the subject. In questions we often use ever instead of always.

  • Do you usually walk to work?
  • Does she often visit her family?
  • Do you ever cook Afghan food? — (ever = at any time)

Asking how often

To ask about frequency directly, we use How often…?

  • How often do you go to the market? — Twice a week.
  • How often does he call you? — Every day.

5Adverbs that can move

Some adverbs can also go at the beginning or the end of a sentence. This does not change the meaning much, but it can add a little emphasis.[2]

These can moveExamples
sometimes, usually, often, occasionally, frequently Sometimes I walk to work.
I walk to work sometimes.
Usually we have class on Friday.
always, never, rarely, seldom, hardly ever These normally stay in the middle.
Say: I never eat meat.  Not: I eat meat never.

6Frequency expressions

These are phrases, not single adverbs. They go at the beginning or, more usually, the end of the sentence — never in the middle.

ExpressionExample
every day / every weekI study English every day.
once a weekWe have class once a week.
twice a monthShe visits her sister twice a month.
three times a yearThey travel three times a year.
on Fridays / on weekendsOn Fridays I cook for my family.
Not correctI twice a week go to the market.
CorrectI go to the market twice a week.

7Common mistakes

Not correctCorrect
She drinks always tea.She always drinks tea.
He always is late.He is always late.
I don't never go out.I never go out.
I go never to the market.I never go to the market.
She usually don't work late.She doesn't usually work late.
I every day study English.I study English every day.

8Quick summary

SituationWhere the adverb goes
Main verbBefore the verb — She always drinks tea.
The verb beAfter the verb — She is always tired.
Helping verbAfter the first helping verb — I have never been there.
NegativeAfter don't / doesn't — I don't usually eat out.
QuestionAfter the subject — Do you usually walk?
ExpressionsBeginning or end — I go there twice a week.
Remember Most adverbs of frequency go before the main verb — but after the verb be. If you remember only one thing today, remember that.
